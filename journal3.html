Why This Journal Exists

Thoughts do not always arrive as sentences. Some show up as shapes, scenes, symbols or quick marks in the margin. This journal gives both sides of the creative process a place to live, so you can follow an idea instead of forcing it into one format.

Words • Lines • Connection

A three-step practice for seeing an idea from two directions

1
✍

Words

Start on a lined page. Write what is on your mind without trying to make it polished. Describe the thought, problem, memory or idea as plainly as you can.

2
✎

Lines

Turn to a blank page and represent the same idea without relying on sentences. Sketch an object, scene, symbol, map, shape or pattern that captures how the idea feels.

3
↔

Connection

Look at the writing and the drawing together. Notice what appeared in one that did not appear in the other. Write one sentence about the connection you can see now.

6 Prompts for Words and Blank Pages

Try each prompt once in writing and once as a sketch

1

Write about where your attention keeps going today, then sketch it as a place.

4

Describe something you want more of, then draw the feeling rather than the object.

2

List three things competing for your energy, then turn them into shapes on a blank page.

5

Write about a decision you are considering, then sketch two possible paths forward.

3

Write one memory in five sentences, then draw the single detail you remember most clearly.

6

Describe how today feels in words, then give that feeling a symbol you can recognize later.

Sometimes the words explain the picture. Sometimes the picture explains the words.
