A journaling technique to try

The Notice • Name • Next Method

A simple three-step practice for understanding what is happening inside you, finding clarity and turning reflection into one small meaningful action.

1

Notice

Start by noticing what is on your mind right now. What happened today? What keeps replaying? What emotion is sitting closest to the surface? Write without trying to fix or edit anything yet.

2

Name

Give the thought or feeling a name. Are you grateful, overwhelmed, hopeful, unsure, disappointed, energized or relieved? Naming what is present can make it easier to understand and less likely to stay tangled in your head.

3

Next

Ask yourself: What is one small, kind next step I can take? It does not have to be dramatic. It might be sending a message, protecting thirty quiet minutes, finishing one task, asking a question, or simply choosing to let something go.

Try this: A 5-Minute Journal Practice
Set a timer for five minutes. Write about what is on your mind using Notice • Name • Next. Do not edit, do not overthink and do not worry about handwriting or grammar. Five honest minutes can be a complete journal entry.
Journal entry example

What five honest minutes can look like

“I noticed I have been feeling anxious about everything on my to-do list. I think I am overwhelmed because I want to do so much and I am afraid I will fall behind. My next step is to choose just one task for today and focus on completing it. I can do hard things, one step at a time.”

When you do not know what to write

6 journal prompts to get started

  1. What made me smile today?
  2. What am I grateful for right now?
  3. What is something I am looking forward to?
  4. What has been on my mind lately?
  5. What is one small step I can take toward a goal?
  6. What do I want to remember about this season of my life?
Make it a habit

A simple weekly reflection

At the end of each week, take a few minutes to look back through your entries. Notice repeated feelings, people, worries, wins or ideas. Ask yourself what gave you energy, what drained it and what deserves more attention next week.
