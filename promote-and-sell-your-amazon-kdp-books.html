What the book helps you do

Build a promotion system instead of hoping readers find you

The book is designed around a simple progression: strengthen the free foundation first, add paid promotion only when the book is ready, then turn what works into a repeatable system you can use across your backlist.

  • Choose categories and keywords more strategically.
  • Build reviews with an advance reader team.

Promote, Measure & Grow

A few ideas to put to work now

Start with the free foundation before you buy traffic

One of the book’s central ideas is that promotion works best when your listing, reader targeting and trust signals are already strong. Paid promotion should amplify something that is working—not be used to discover basic problems the expensive way.

1.

Audit the listing that does the selling

Look at your cover at thumbnail size, your title and subtitle, the first lines of your description, your reviews and your sample. Promotion can bring people to the page, but the page still has to earn the sale.

2.

Define the reader before choosing channels

Build a useful reader profile from real reviews, questions and communities. Then identify specific places that reader already spends time instead of trying to promote everywhere.

3.

Choose one organic channel you can sustain

Consistency on one relevant platform usually beats a scattered presence across several. Extract useful ideas from the book itself and build a realistic posting rhythm around them.

Try this: Pick one currently published book and write down four numbers: recent sales trend, review count, page views versus sales, and any ad cost of sale. Then identify the weakest link before spending money on more traffic.
4.

Build an audience you own

Create a useful lead magnet and simple email welcome sequence. An email list gives you a direct way to reach readers during launches without depending entirely on a social platform’s algorithm.

5.

Turn promotion into a routine

Document the actions that work, schedule the repeatable ones, review the important numbers weekly and keep the human parts—relationships, replies and community engagement—personal.
