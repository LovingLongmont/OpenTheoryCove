Why this book exists

Make the difficult moments a little easier to navigate.

When someone is ill, injured, unavailable or has died, family members can suddenly be left trying to answer dozens of practical questions while they are already under stress. Where are important records? Who needs to be contacted? What accounts, household information or instructions should they know about?

If Something Happens To Me gives you a dedicated place to gather information intentionally instead of leaving loved ones to piece it together later.

A useful choice for people who want to…

✓ Organize important information before an emergency

✓ Give a spouse, adult child or trusted person a practical reference

✓ Reduce the amount of searching family members may need to do

✓ Start conversations about preparedness without making them overwhelming

✓ Create a central record that can be reviewed and updated over time

From the Open Theory Cove journal

Why writing down your “just in case” information matters

Most of us carry an enormous amount of everyday knowledge in our heads. We know which bill is paid automatically, where a document is stored, which company handles a service, who to call about the house and which details would matter if another person suddenly had to step into our shoes. Because that knowledge feels ordinary to us, it is easy to underestimate how difficult it would be for someone else to reconstruct.

Preparedness is really about reducing uncertainty

Planning for an emergency does not require predicting what will happen. The useful part is identifying information another person would struggle to find without you. A written organizer can turn scattered knowledge into a roadmap. That can save time, reduce repeated phone calls and give a trusted person somewhere to begin.

Writing creates a different kind of clarity

Putting information on paper forces us to notice gaps. You may realize that an important contact is only stored in your phone, that nobody else knows where a document is kept, or that instructions you thought were obvious have never actually been explained. The act of writing can therefore be as valuable as the finished organizer.

Make it a living record, not a one-time project

The most useful preparedness organizer is one you can actually maintain. Set aside time periodically to review what has changed. Keep the book somewhere secure, tell an appropriate trusted person how it can be accessed, and avoid recording sensitive information in a way that creates unnecessary security risk. The goal is not to document every detail of your life. It is to leave enough of a map that the people you trust are not starting from zero.

This book is an organizational aid and is not a substitute for legal, financial, medical or estate-planning advice.
