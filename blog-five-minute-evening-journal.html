Daily Reflection

A Five-Minute Evening Journal Practice

You do not need to fill pages to get something useful from journaling. The Notice • Name • Next method is a short evening practice for days when your mind is full but you are not sure where to begin.

1. Notice

Choose one moment from the day that stayed with you. Describe what happened before you explain it. A conversation, a frustration, a small win, a quiet moment, or something you kept thinking about all count.

2. Name

Give your reaction a name. Were you relieved, disappointed, proud, anxious, grateful or tired? Naming the feeling can make a vague reaction easier to understand.

3. Next

Finish with one small next step, intention or question for tomorrow. It might be an action, a boundary, a reminder, or simply something you want to notice again.

Try this tonight: Set a five-minute timer. Write one sentence for Notice, one for Name and one for Next. Stop when the timer ends. A complete journal entry can be that simple.

Look for patterns, not perfect entries

Once a week, read back through several entries. Ask: What has been giving me energy? What has been draining it? What keeps repeating? The usefulness of journaling often appears in the patterns between days, not in any single page.
